Demo Day Runbook
PAPEL · Research Archiving System · PUP Biñan Campus
What to prepare before the defence, and how to show each workflow

For the capstone defence · demonstrated on the live site, pupbcpapel.com

This is the preparation list, not the handbook. It answers one question: what has to exist before we stand up, so that every workflow can be shown without waiting, apologising, or typing something that fails in front of the panel.

Read Part 1 the day before. Everything in it takes time that you will not have on the day.

How to use this document

Every workflow in Part 3 is written the same way:
Needs What must already exist. If this is missing, the demo stops.
Show What to click, in order.
They see What should appear, so you know it worked.
Say The sentence that explains why it is built that way. A panel asks why, not what.
Again How to put it back, to run the same demo twice.

This is the live site. Every account you create sends a real email. Every notice a workflow raises reaches a real inbox. Use addresses you own, keep to papers whose titles start with DEMO, and never demonstrate on a real student's work.

Part 1 · The day before

1.1 Run the two database updates

Without these, two things in the demo simply do not work.

Open phpMyAdmin on the production hosting, select the site's database on the left, open the SQL tab, paste each file whole, and press Go. Run them in this order. Each one prints a line saying what it did, and each is safe to run twice.

Order File If you skip it
1 scripts/migrations/sql/2026-11-15_request_history_visibility.sql A student cannot clear their request history; My Library may error.
2 scripts/migrations/sql/2026-11-23_manuscript_file_hash.sql Workflow 3b cannot be demonstrated at all. The duplicate check has nothing to compare a PDF against.

These files are not on the server. They are in the project folder on your own computer, under scripts/migrations/sql/. Open each in Notepad, select all, copy.

Then check it worked. Sign in as the Research Coordinator or the Director and open:

pupbcpapel.com/app/student/upload_diagnostics.php

The Database section must read "No gaps". If it lists anything, the paste did not take. Do not proceed until it is clean.

1.2 Check Google Drive

Sign in as the Director or the Research Coordinator and open Storage Folder. Confirm all three:

  • Drive says connected, and names the Google account.
  • The storage folder is usable by that account, in so many words.
  • The folder opens in Drive when you press Open folder.

If the folder is not usable, no paper can be submitted at all and Workflows 3, 6, 7 and 8 are dead. This is the single most common cause of a failed demo: the connection and the folder are two separate settings and they fail separately.

Three things to check about them, not assume

1. The student must be on that adviser's roll. This is the one thing that can quietly break the review demo. A paper does not go to "an adviser"; the desk lists the students whose accounts that adviser created (users.created_by), and a paper by anybody else is in no queue at all. It is not visible from the student's side, so check it from the adviser's.

Sign in as Kris Rommel Ramos and open My Students. The student you chose must be on that list. If the list is empty, or your student is missing, pick a student that is on it and use that account instead.

If you would rather check it in phpMyAdmin, this answers it in one line and writes nothing:

SELECT user_id, full_name, student_id FROM users
 WHERE user_role = 'student' AND created_by = 384;

384 is Kris Rommel Ramos. Whatever comes back is exactly what his desk can see. If it comes back empty, the students were created by somebody else, and Workflow 6 has to be demonstrated from whichever account did create them.

2. The student account must not have expired. All fifteen expire on 31 July 2028, so this is not a risk tomorrow. It is still worth knowing why: the life of a student account is counted from the section on it, and an expired account cannot sign in at all. Moving a student up a year is how one is renewed.

3. You must actually hold five passwords. These are real accounts and the passwords are not in this document. Four of the five belong to other people; if you are borrowing them, arrange it tonight and confirm each one signs in.

Do not plan to reset anybody's password in the morning. A password reset by somebody else holds that account on a change your password screen until it is replaced, and nothing else on the site opens: you would be changing a colleague's password in front of the panel.

A real desk shows real work. The Review Desk of a working adviser lists other students' names and the titles of papers that are not published. The Coordinator's desk and the support queue are the same. Decide in advance whether that is acceptable to project, and if it is not, use the desk's own filters to bring your demo paper up before you share the screen.

1.4 Prepare the PDF files

You need six different PDF files on the laptop, in one folder, named so you can find them under pressure:

demo-01-imrad.pdf        demo-04-returned.pdf
demo-02-full.pdf         demo-05-ethics.pdf
demo-03-duplicate.pdf    demo-06-consent.pdf

They must genuinely be different files. PAPEL now refuses a PDF whose contents are already on file, and it knows a file by what is inside it, so copying one and renaming it is still the same file and will be refused. This is a feature you are going to demonstrate; do not let it ambush you first.

demo-03-duplicate.pdf is the exception: it is a copy of demo-01-imrad.pdf under another name, and it exists only so you can show the duplicate check catching it.

1.5 Stage the papers

One paper cannot be in two states at once, so the demo needs several, each parked where a workflow needs it. Upload them as the student from 1.3, so they land on Kris Rommel Ramos's desk, and give every one of them a title beginning DEMO so the reset script can find them and nothing else can be touched by it. Prepare them in this order, the day before.

Part 2 · Running order

Do not demo the workflows in numerical order. This order tells one story, and each step leaves the system ready for the next.

  1. The shape of it — show the Public Repository signed out. One sentence on what PAPEL is for.
  2. Signing in (1) as the student.
  3. Uploading (3), then the duplicate check (3b).
  4. Drafts (4) and withdrawing (5).
  5. Adviser review (6) → Coordinator publishes (7).
  6. Returned paper (8).
  7. Reading (9), manuscript request (11), guest pass (10).
  8. Archiving (12).
  9. Notices (13) and support (14).
  10. Accounts (2) last, because it sends email and is the least visual.

Part 3 · The workflows

Workflow 1 · Signing In

Needs — the student account from 1.3, and its password in your hand.

Show — press Sign In, choose the Student tab, type the ID and password, tick the robot box, sign in.

They see — the student dashboard.

Say — "The robot check and the lock are tested before the password is ever looked at, so this form cannot be used to try passwords. Five wrong tries lock that account for fifteen minutes."

Again — sign out.

Workflow 2 · Creating User Accounts

Needs — the Subject Adviser signed in. Decide beforehand which of the two ways you are doing this, because one of them makes a real account.

Show, without creating anything — My Students → fill the form in → press Generate and let it build the password from the name and the ID → stop there and explain what Create would do. Then show Import beside the search box and the spreadsheet preview, which checks every row without creating anything until you press Create.

Or, if you would rather show it end to end — use an email address you own, create one account, and delete it immediately afterwards. It is a real account on the real roll until you do.

They see — the generated password, and the rules answering under each box as it is typed in.

Say — "The password is generated from the name and the ID, and it is temporary: the first time they sign in, PAPEL holds them on one page until they choose their own, because a password that was emailed has been seen by somebody else."

Again — if you created one, delete it now rather than later.

Mention the spreadsheet import even if you do not run it. Forty students at the start of a term is the real case, and the preview checks every row by the same rules as typing one in.

Workflow 3 · Uploading a Paper

Needs — the student signed in; demo-01-imrad.pdf and demo-02-full.pdf; Drive connected.

Show — Upload Paper. Walk the four steps. On Step 1 tick both IMRAD and Full Manuscript so there are two file boxes. Attach a different PDF to each. On Step 2 press Extract with AI. On Step 2 also show the question that asks which written sections go on the paper's page. Submit.

They see — the paper filed, the dashboard showing ON PROCESS.

Say — "Both ticked is one paper with two files, not two papers. And nothing reaches Google Drive until every check has passed, so a refusal costs the student no waiting."

Again — the paper is now with the adviser; use it for Workflow 6, or have the adviser return it.

Workflow 3b · The duplicate check ← new, and worth showing

Needs — step 1.1 done; DEMO Published Paper filed after it; demo-03-duplicate.pdf, which is a renamed copy of a PDF already filed.

Show — start a new upload, fill the required boxes, attach demo-03-duplicate.pdf, and submit.

They see — a refusal in words: the PDF is already filed, naming the student's own paper.

Say — "It compares what is inside the file, not its name, so renaming it changes nothing. Two students really do both call a file final.pdf, and a renamed copy of a filed paper is still that paper. Resubmitting a returned paper with the same file is allowed, because that is a revision, not a duplicate."

Again — nothing was filed, so nothing to undo. It can be run as often as you like.

Workflow 4 · Saving a Draft

Needs — DEMO Draft Paper in Drafts.

Show — open the draft from the dashboard, show the typing restored, point at the file boxes being empty.

Say — "A draft keeps everything typed but no PDF, on purpose: nothing is stored for work nobody has handed in. Signing out also clears the copy kept in this browser, because on a campus machine the next person is somebody else."

Again — nothing changes.

Workflow 5 · Cancelling a Submission

Needs — DEMO Waiting Adviser, submitted less than 24 hours ago and still with the adviser.

Show — the dashboard → Under Review → Withdraw.

They see — the paper back in Drafts.

Say — "The two stages work in opposite directions. With the adviser it is allowed for the first 24 hours, a grace period for second thoughts. Once the adviser has approved it, withdrawing unlocks only after the Coordinator has held it a day: the paper has earned an approval and is not pulled back on a whim."

Again — submit it again. Note it will then be a fresh 24-hour window.

Workflow 6 · Review by the Subject Adviser

Needs — a paper waiting with the adviser; the adviser signed in.

Show — Review Desk → open the paper → the PDF beside the form → the checklist → Edit sections → Approve.

They see — the paper leaves the desk and reaches the Coordinator.

Say — "The checklist is stored as the adviser left it, so changing the ready-made lists in code never rewrites what a student was shown. The Coordinator reads this list but cannot change it."

Again — the Coordinator can return it, which sends it back to the student.

Workflow 7 · Final Review and Publishing

Needs — DEMO Waiting Coordinator; the Coordinator signed in.

Show — Review Desk → open → Approve → then open the Public Repository and find it.

Say — "Approval ends here, deliberately. The Head of Academic Programs and the Director read what comes out; they do not approve. A fourth step was considered and cut."

Again — the Director can archive it, or you can leave it published.

Workflow 8 · Fixing a Returned Paper

Needs — DEMO Returned Paper in the student's Needs Revision.

Show — open it, read the feedback on the card, fix something, attach the PDFs again, submit.

Say — "Its files were taken out of the storage folder when it was returned, so the new version needs a new upload. They are not destroyed: they are moved to a Trash folder inside the storage folder, because an adviser who returns a paper by mistake must not destroy the student's only filed copy."

Again — have the adviser return it once more.

Workflow 9 · Finding and Reading Published Papers

Needs — at least one published paper. Best shown signed out first.

Show — the Public Repository: search, the keyword strip, the filters. Then the four views in the profile menu: Cards, Rows, Hardbounds, Bookshelf. Open a paper. Press the bookmark, then open My Library.

Say — "The repository is the point of the system: work that was reviewed, kept where the next batch can find it. The shelf views exist because a list of twenty-seven rows does not look like a library."

Again — press the bookmark again to unsave.

Workflow 10 · Guest Passes

Needs — the Librarian account from 1.3; an email address you can open.

Show — Guest Passes → create one → open the email → sign in on the Guest tab in a private window.

Say — "A pass is for a visiting researcher or a panel member who has no account. It lasts from one to twenty-four hours and then stops working by itself."

Again — create another; delete the old one.

Workflow 11 · Asking to Open a Full Manuscript

Needs — DEMO Published Paper; the student with no open request on it; the Librarian signed in in a second window.

The student may hold three requests at once, and a paper with one still open cannot be asked about again. If you have practised this, clear it first: the reset SQL in the appendix does it.

Show — as the student, open the paper, press to request the manuscript. Switch to the Librarian: the request is waiting. Grant it for an hour. Switch back: the countdown appears in the corner. Open the PDF and point at the watermark. Then have the Librarian revoke it.

Say — "The reader's name is burnt into the page as it is drawn, so any copy, even a photograph of the screen, names the account it came from. We cannot stop a screenshot; no web page can. What we can do is make every copy traceable."

Again — the student may request again at once after a revoke.

A student may hold three active requests at a time. Do not use up the demo student's allowance the day before.

Workflow 12 · Archiving a Paper

Needs — DEMO Published Paper, published; the Director signed in.

Show — the Director's desk → Archived tab → archive the demo paper → show it is gone from the Public Repository → restore it.

Say — "Archiving is a database move, not a file one. The PDFs on Drive are not touched at all, the paper is still readable by its own link, and restoring puts it straight back. The five-year rule is written and tested; nothing schedules it yet, which is why we are doing it by hand."

Again — restore it, so the repository is as you found it.

Only ever archive a DEMO paper. Archiving a real student's paper removes it from the repository for everyone.

Workflow 13 · Notifications and Emails

Needs — any of the earlier workflows already run, so notices exist.

Show — the bell, the notification centre, then the matching email in the inbox.

Say — "Every notice is both a bell and an email, from one call, so the two can never disagree. The subject of the email is also the heading printed inside it."

Again — nothing to reset.

Workflow 14 · Getting Help with an Account

Needs — nothing. Best shown signed out.

Show — Contact Support → send one → sign in as the Coordinator and show the queue.

Say — "It needs no account, which is the whole point: it is for the person who cannot sign in."

Again — close the request from the queue.

Part 4 · Say these before you are asked

A panel trusts a team that volunteers the limits. Each of these is a real boundary, and each has a reason.

  • A screenshot cannot be blocked. No web page can stop PrintScreen, a recorder, or a phone camera; the blackout people have seen on video sites is a hardware path open only to video. We mark every page with the reader's name instead, so a copy can be traced. Prevention was not available; traceability was.
  • Nothing schedules the five-year archive yet. The rule works and was driven by hand against a seeded six-year-old paper. Starting it is one cron entry.
  • The PDF compressor is switched off at the owner's instruction. The size limit does not depend on it: a file over the limit is refused and the student compresses it themselves.
  • The AI can be wrong. Extract with AI fills the metadata and copies the abstract only when it can find it exactly; the student checks every field. A wrong abstract is worse than none, so when it cannot find one it leaves the box alone.
  • Staff papers skip the review and publish at once. That is deliberate: the chain exists to review students' work.
  • One person may approve twice if they are both the Subject Adviser and the Research Coordinator. The owner chose that; for those papers the second step adds nothing.

Part 5 · Questions the panel will probably ask

Question The short answer
Why only two approval steps? It was shortened on purpose. The Head and the Director read what comes out; a fourth step added delay and no judgement.
Where are the files actually kept? One Google Drive folder, chosen by the Director or the Coordinator. Never on the web server; the local copy is deleted as soon as Drive has it.
What stops two students uploading the same paper? The contents are fingerprinted. A renamed copy is still the same file. Resubmitting your own returned paper is allowed.
What if Google Drive is down? Submission is refused in words before anything is stored, rather than half-filing a paper.
Is anything deleted? Almost nothing. A returned paper's files move to a Trash folder; archiving only moves a database row; a revoked grant keeps its record.
How do you stop a student seeing another student's paper? A paper under review is visible only to its author and its reviewers. The full PDF of a published paper needs a Librarian's grant.
What about passwords? Stored hashed, never in plain text. A password set by somebody else is temporary and the account is held until it is changed.
Does it work on a phone? Yes. The layout changes at 900px and the upload form becomes one column.
What happens when the account expires? A student account's life comes from the section, counted in academic years and ending 31 July. Moving a student up a year renews it.

Part 6 · If something goes wrong

It happens Do this
A page shows Something Went Wrong Do not retry in front of them. Say "that is the error page, it carries a reference and the cause is logged", and move to the next workflow.
The upload fails Open Storage Folder and read the folder check. This is almost always Drive, not the paper.
You are signed out mid-demo The session idles after 30 minutes. Sign in again; it takes ten seconds.
The duplicate check does not fire The SQL in 1.1 was not run, or the paper you are testing against was filed before it.
A paper is stuck in the wrong state Use the reset SQL in the appendix.
The AI does not answer Say so plainly and fill the fields manually. The AI is a convenience, not the system.

Have a second browser window already signed in as the adviser. Switching roles by signing out and in again is the slowest thing in the whole demo.

Appendix · Resetting the demo data

docs/demo/demo_reset.sql puts the demo papers back to their starting states so the whole run can be done again. Paste it into phpMyAdmin exactly as you pasted the files in 1.1.

It only ever touches rows whose title begins with DEMO, and it changes no real student's work. Read the comments at the top before running it; it tells you what it will do and prints what it did.

It cannot create papers, because a paper needs a real PDF on Google Drive. Papers are made by uploading them, as in 1.5.
