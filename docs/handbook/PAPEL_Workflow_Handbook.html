About This Handbook

This handbook explains how PAPEL works, step by step.

PAPEL is the research archiving system of PUP Biñan Campus. Students upload their research papers. Teachers check them. Approved papers are shared with the public. Later, old papers can be archived.

Who should read it

Students, Research Advisers, the Research Coordinator, the Head of Academic Programs, the Director, Librarians and guests. Also the people who look after the system: they will find notes for them in Appendix D.

How each workflow is arranged

  1. In short: one sentence about the workflow.
  2. Who takes part: the people, and any outside service.
  3. The steps: what to do, in order.
  4. Remember: the important rules.
  5. Words to know: new words, explained simply.
  6. Three diagrams: a use case diagram (who does what), a sequence diagram (what happens behind the screen) and an ERD (where the information is saved).
Good to know

You do not need the diagrams to use PAPEL. The steps are enough. The diagrams are for people who want to see how PAPEL works inside. Start Here 3 explains how to read them.

How words are written

  • Words in bold are the words you see on the screen, like Submit Paper.
  • Words in this style are names used inside the system, like the status draft. You do not need them to use PAPEL.
  • "Workflow 5" means chapter 5 of this handbook.

How to Read the Diagrams

Each workflow has three diagrams. They follow UML, a standard way of drawing software. Here is how to read them.

1. The use case diagram: who does what

  • A stick figure is a person. It is called an actor.
  • A blue box marked «system» is an outside service, like Google Drive.
  • The big pink box is PAPEL. Everything inside it happens in PAPEL.
  • An oval is one thing a person can do. It is called a use case.
  • A plain line means: this person does this.
  • A dashed arrow marked «include» means: this step always happens as part of the other.
  • A dashed arrow marked «extend» means: this step happens only sometimes.

2. The sequence diagram: what happens, in order

  • The boxes at the top are the people and the parts of the system. The maroon box is PAPEL. The cylinder is the database. Blue dashed boxes are outside services.
  • The dashed line going down from each box is its lifeline. Time goes down.
  • A solid arrow is a request or an action. The number shows the order.
  • A dashed arrow is an answer coming back.
  • A loop arrow means the part does something by itself.
  • An alt box has two or more paths. Only one happens. The words in [brackets] say when.
  • An opt box happens only sometimes. A loop box repeats.
  • A yellow note explains something.

3. The ERD: where the information is saved

  • ERD means Entity Relationship Diagram. Each box is one table in the database. The dark top shows the table's name.
  • Each line in the box is one column: one piece of information.
  • PK (primary key) is the ID that makes each row different.
  • FK (foreign key) is an ID that points to a row in another table.
  • Two short bars || at the end of a line mean one. The three-toed crow's foot means many. The example reads: one user uploads many papers.
  • Each workflow shows only the columns it uses. "+ 20 more columns" tells you the rest are hidden. Appendix A has the full map.

The Big Picture

A paper moves through PAPEL in five stages.

  1. The student writes the paper and uploads it.
  2. The Research Adviser checks it.
  3. The Research Coordinator checks it again and approves it.
  4. The paper is published in the Public Repository. Everyone can find it.
  5. Later, the Director can move it to the archive.

At stage 2 or stage 3, the reviewer can return the paper. The student fixes it and sends it again. It then starts again at the Research Adviser.

The status of a paper

The status says where a paper is in the process. Your dashboard shows it as a badge on the paper's card.

Steps

  1. Click Sign In at the top of the page.
  2. Choose your tab: Student, Faculty or Guest.
  3. Type your ID.
  4. Type your password.
  5. Tick I'm not a robot, if you see it.
  6. Click the sign-in button.
  7. PAPEL opens your home page. (See the table of people in Start Here 2.)
Remember
  • Five wrong passwords lock the account for 15 minutes. This stops people who try to guess passwords. Wait 15 minutes, then try again.
  • Twenty wrong tries from one computer lock that computer for 15 minutes, for every account.
  • Choose the right tab. A staff account cannot sign in on the Student tab.
  • A student account has an end date. After that date it stops working. Ask your Research Adviser to renew it.
  • Forgot your password? Use Contact Support. See Workflow 14.

How long a student account lasts

The end date comes from your year level and section. PAPEL counts school years from the academic year on your account. The account ends on 31 July of the last school year.

Steps: a Research Adviser makes a student account

  1. Open My Students.
  2. Fill in the form: full name, Student ID, email, program, section and academic year.
  3. Click Generate to make a password. It is made from the name and the ID, for example JuanDelaCruz-056.

Remember
  • A password needs at least 6 characters, one capital letter and one number.
  • Tell new users to change their password after they sign in for the first time.
  • To renew a student, edit the account and move the student up a year.
  • PAPEL records every password change: whose password, who changed it, and when.

WORKFLOW 03 · PART B · SUBMITTING A PAPER

Uploading a Paper

In shortThe student fills in four steps and submits the paper. It goes to their Research Adviser.

Who takes partStudentResearch Adviser (gets a notice)
Outside servicesGroq AIGoogle DriveGmail

Open Upload Paper from your dashboard. The upload page has four steps. You can go back to an earlier step at any time.

Step 1: Paper Details

  • Choose your academic program, the paper type and the other basic facts.
  • Paper types: Capstone Project, Undergraduate Thesis, Conference Paper, Journal Article and Feasibility Study.

Step 2: Upload & Details

  • Attach your paper as a PDF file. The page accepts files up to 50 MB.
  • Choose how to fill in the details:
  • Extract Metadata with AI: PAPEL reads your PDF. It fills in the title, authors, year, keywords and abstract. If the AI is busy, wait a minute and press it again, or fill the details in yourself.
  • Fill Manually: you type the details yourself.
  • Always check what the AI wrote. Fix any mistakes.
  • Write or paste each section of your paper: Abstract, Introduction, Methodology, Results and Discussion, Conclusion, References.
  • You can paste pictures into a section. Each picture can be up to 5 MB.

Step 3: Supporting Docs

A Capstone Project needs all four documents below. For the other paper types, they are optional. Every document must be a PDF.

  • Ethics Clearance
  • Consent Form
  • Data Collection Tool
  • Copyright / IP Document

Step 4: Review & Submit

Check everything one last time. Then click Submit Paper.

What happens after you click Submit Paper

  1. PAPEL checks that every required part is there.
  2. PAPEL sends your PDF and your documents to Google Drive. Submitted papers are kept there.
  3. PAPEL saves the paper. The badge on your card says ON PROCESS.
  4. Your Research Adviser gets a notice and an email.
  5. You go back to your dashboard. The paper is in the Under Review tab.
Remember
  • Your paper always goes first to your Research Adviser: the faculty member who made your account.
  • If the upload to Google Drive fails, the paper is not submitted. Try again later.
  • You can save your work before you submit. See Workflow 4.
  • You can cancel a submission for a short time. See Workflow 5.
  • Staff can upload their own papers too. A staff paper is published at once, with no review.
Words to know
PDF
A file type for documents. It looks the same on every computer.
Metadata
Basic facts about a paper, like the title, authors and year.
Submit
Send your paper for review.

WORKFLOW 05 · PART B · SUBMITTING A PAPER

Cancelling a Submission

In shortYou can take back a submitted paper, but only at certain times.

Who takes partStudentResearch Adviser and Research Coordinator (get a notice)

After you submit, your paper card shows a Cancel submission button when it is allowed. Cancelling brings the paper back to you as a draft.

Where is your paper?When can you cancel?
With your Research AdviserOnly in the first 24 hours after you submit.
With the Research CoordinatorOnly after the Coordinator has had it for 24 hours with no decision.
PublishedYou cannot cancel.

Why is it different? At the first stage, 24 hours give you time to change your mind. After that, the Adviser should not review a paper that keeps changing. At the second stage, your paper already has an approval. So you can take it back only if it waits too long.

Steps

  1. Open your dashboard. Go to the Under Review tab.
  2. Find the paper.
  3. Click Cancel submission.
  4. A box asks Withdraw this submission? Click Withdraw it.
  5. The paper goes back to your Drafts.
  6. Your reviewers get a notice.
Remember
  • Approvals that were already given stay on record.
  • If the time has passed, the card tells you why. Ask your Research Adviser for help.
  • If a reviewer acts at the same moment as you, the reviewer's decision wins.
  • To send the paper again, open the draft and submit it. It starts again with your Research Adviser.
Words to know
Cancel / Withdraw

Window

WORKFLOW 06 · PART C · REVIEW AND APPROVAL

Review by the Research Adviser

In shortThe Research Adviser checks the paper first. They approve it or return it.

Who takes partResearch AdviserStudentResearch Coordinator (gets a notice)
Outside servicesGoogle DriveGmail

The Research Adviser works on the Review Desk. It has four tabs.

TabWhat is in it
Waiting for youPapers you must review now.
With CoordinatorPapers you approved. They wait for the Research Coordinator.
PublishedYour students' published papers.
ReturnedPapers you sent back.

You see papers from your own students only: the students whose accounts you made.

Steps: approve a paper

  1. Open the paper in Waiting for you. Read it.
  2. Click Approve and forward. A checklist opens.
  3. Tick each part the paper has. For example: Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion and References. Or: Chapter 1 to Chapter 5 and References.
  4. You can leave the Note for the student box empty. (Today, no page shows this note to the student.)
  5. Confirm with Approve and forward.
  6. The paper goes to the Research Coordinator. The Coordinator gets a notice and an email.

Steps: return a paper

  1. Open the paper. Click Return with feedback.
  2. A box asks Return this paper?
  3. Write What needs to change. Be clear, so the student knows what to fix.
  4. Click Return to student.
  5. The paper goes back to the student. Their dashboard shows it under Needs Revision.
  6. The student gets a notice and an email with your feedback.
Remember
  • Feedback is required when you return a paper. PAPEL refuses feedback that is empty, looks like random letters, or has rude words.
  • When you return a paper, its files are deleted from Google Drive. The student must attach the PDF again.
  • Your checklist is saved with the paper.
  • You can act only on papers that are waiting for you.
Words to know
Approve
Say yes. The paper moves on.
Return
Send the paper back to the student to fix.
Feedback
Your comments about what to change.

Final Review and Publishing

In shortThe Research Coordinator makes the final decision. Approving a paper publishes it.

Who takes partResearch CoordinatorStudentHead of Academic Programs and Director (read)
Outside servicesGoogle DriveGmail

The Research Coordinator also works on a Review Desk. It has four tabs: Waiting for you, With Advisers, Published and Returned. The Coordinator sees papers from all programs.

Steps: approve and publish

  1. Open the paper in Waiting for you.
  2. Read the paper and the Adviser's checklist.
  3. Click Approve and publish.
  4. Confirm with Approve and publish. (The Note for the student box is not saved at this step.)

Steps: return a paper

This works the same way as for the Adviser (Workflow 6). Click Return with feedback, write What needs to change, then click Return to student. The files are deleted from Google Drive. The student gets a notice. When the student sends it again, it starts again at the Research Adviser.

WORKFLOW 08 · PART C · REVIEW AND APPROVAL

Fixing a Returned Paper

In shortIf a reviewer returns your paper, read the feedback, fix the paper and submit it again.

Who takes partStudentResearch Adviser (gets it again)
Outside servicesGoogle Drive

A returned paper shows in the Needs Revision tab of your dashboard. Its badge says DECLINED. The card shows Revision feedback: the reviewer's words.

Good to know

"Returned" does not mean "rejected forever". It means: please fix it and send it again.

Steps

  1. Open the Needs Revision tab.
  2. Read the Revision feedback carefully.
  3. Click Edit and Re-submit.
  4. Fix your paper, as the feedback says.
  5. Attach your PDF again. The old files were deleted from Google Drive when the paper was returned.
  6. Attach the supporting documents again, if your paper type needs them.
  7. Click Submit Paper.
  8. The paper goes to your Research Adviser again, even if the Research Coordinator was the one who returned it.
Remember
  • Old feedback stays on record. Reviewers can see the history.
  • You can delete a returned paper if you do not want to continue.
  • Nobody can see your changes until you submit again.
Words to know
Revision
A change to make your work better.
Re-submit

Guest Passes

In shortA Librarian gives a visitor a pass that works for a few hours.

Who takes partLibrarianGuest
Outside servicesGmail

A guest is a visitor without a PAPEL account. A guest pass lets them read paper details for a short time. The Research Coordinator and the Director can also open this page.

Steps: the Librarian makes a pass

  1. Open Guest Passes.
  2. Type the guest's email address.
  3. Choose how long the pass works: 1, 2, 4, 8, 12 or 24 hours.
  4. Create the pass.
  5. PAPEL checks that the email does not belong to a PAPEL account.
  6. PAPEL makes a username, like guest_3fa9c21b, and a 12-character password.
  7. PAPEL emails them to the guest. You can also see them on the screen.

Steps: the guest signs in

  1. Open the email from PAPEL.
  2. Go to PAPEL and click Sign In.
  3. Choose the Guest tab.
  4. Type the username and the password.
  5. Read the Public Repository.
  6. When the time ends, PAPEL signs you out.
Remember
  • A guest can read paper details. A guest cannot open the full PDF.
  • A pass cannot be made for an email that already has a PAPEL account, even if it is written differently, for example with a dot or a "+" in a Gmail address. That person signs in with their own account.
  • A pass cannot be made longer. To give more time, make a new pass.
  • The Librarian can delete a pass early. Nobody can sign in with it after that.
  • The password is easy to read out loud. It never uses the letters I, l, O or the number 0.
  • Expired passes are listed apart. The Librarian can clear them.
Words to know
Guest
A visitor without a PAPEL account.
Pass

Asking to Open a Full Manuscript

In shortA student asks a Librarian for permission to open a paper's PDF. The Librarian allows it for a few hours, or says no.

Who takes partStudentLibrarian

Students can read the details of every published paper. The full PDF is for staff only. A student who needs the PDF can ask for it.

Steps: the student asks

  1. Open the paper details.
  2. Go to the manuscript part of the page.
  3. Click Request manuscript access.
  4. A message says Request sent. A librarian will review it shortly.
  5. Wait for a notice.

Steps: the Librarian answers

  1. Open Manuscript Requests.
  2. Look at the waiting requests.
  3. To allow it: choose how long, from 1 to 24 hours, and click Grant.

  • If a request was denied, or the access has ended, the student can ask again.
  • Deleting a granted request ends the access at once.
  • Every Librarian gets a notice when a student asks.

WORKFLOW 12 · PART E · RECORDS AND HELP

Archiving a Paper

In shortThe Director can move a published paper out of the Public Repository and into the archive.

Who takes partDirectorThe automatic 5-year task

Archiving is a records decision. It is not part of the review. The paper is not deleted. PAPEL keeps it safely in the archive record.

Steps: the Director archives a paper

  1. Open My Dashboard. It lists all published papers.
  2. Find the paper.
  3. Click Archive.
  4. A box asks Archive this paper? Click Archive it. (Click Leave it published to stop.)
  5. PAPEL copies the whole paper into the archive.
  6. PAPEL removes the paper from the list of active papers.
  7. The paper no longer shows in the Public Repository.

The 5-year rule

PAPEL has a task that archives a published paper by itself when it is more than 5 years old (1,825 days after upload). This task runs only if the system team schedules it to run every day.

Remember
  • Only published (approved) papers can be archived.
  • The archive keeps every part of the paper: the text, the sections, the file link and the details.
  • The paper's files stay in Google Drive.
  • Archived papers still appear in the reports, marked Archived.

APPENDIX B

Common Questions

Short answers to questions people often ask.

Where is my paper now?

Open your dashboard. The tab tells you. Under Review means a reviewer has it. The progress tracker on the card shows who: the Research Adviser or the Research Coordinator.

Why was my paper returned?

Open Needs Revision. The card shows the Revision feedback. It says what to change.

Why must I attach my PDF again after a return?

When a paper is returned, PAPEL deletes its files from Google Drive to save space. So the new version needs a new upload.

I saved my work, but my PDF is gone. Why?

You probably have only the automatic copy. It keeps your typing, but not the PDF. Use Save as Draft to keep the PDF too.

Can I change my paper after I submit it?

Only by cancelling it, and only at the allowed times (Workflow 5). Otherwise, wait for the review. If it is returned, you can change it then.

I cannot sign in. What can I do?

Check that you chose the right tab. Wait 15 minutes if you tried too many times. If your student account expired, ask your Research Adviser. If you forgot your password, use Contact Support (Workflow 14).

Who approves my paper?

Two people: first your Research Adviser, then the Research Coordinator. After the Coordinator approves it, it is published.

Does the Director or the Head of Academic Programs approve papers?

No. They read the published papers. The approval ends at the Research Coordinator.

Why can I not open the full PDF of a published paper?

The full PDF is for staff. Students can ask a Librarian for access for up to 24 hours (Workflow 11).

Is my draft public?

No. Only you can see a draft. Nobody reviews it until you submit it.

How long does a guest pass last?

From 1 to 24 hours. The Librarian chooses when making it.

Is an archived paper deleted?

No. It is kept in the archive record. It is only taken out of the Public Repository.

Things found while writing this handbook

  • The Research Coordinator's approval notice is saved with the type progress (app/models/PaperService.php, approvePaper). progress is not one of the values notifications.notification_type allows. On this XAMPP, where MariaDB is not in strict mode, the notice is saved with a blank type. On a server in strict mode (the MariaDB default), the insert fails silently and the student gets the email but no notice in the bell.
  • The upload page says PDFs may be up to 50 MB, but this XAMPP's php.ini has upload_max_filesize and post_max_size set to 40M. A PDF between 40 and 50 MB fails. Set both to at least 60M on every server.
  • The approve dialog on the Review Desk asks for a "Note for the student" and says it is passed on. The Adviser's note is saved in approval_workflow.feedback, but no page shows it: the student dashboard, paper_details.php and the review console read feedback only from declined rows. The Coordinator's note is not saved at all: admin_review_dashboard.php does not pass it to PaperService::approvePaper.
  • analytics.view_count, download_count and citation_count are never written. Only time_to_approval is (by PaperService::recordTimeToApproval).
  • Five tables are not used by any page: imrad_checklist, paper_favorites, ai_processing_log, storage_usage and gdrive_settings.
